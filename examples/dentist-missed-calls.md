# Business plan — AI after-hours call answering for dental practices

*Built with FILG. Every number is graded by a source-credibility gate. Vendor-marketing stats are labeled, not laundered.*

> **Decisions that shaped this plan (the final branch):** narrowed from "automations for small
> businesses" to **dental practices only**; chose a **B2B retainer** over per-lead pricing; went
> **premium** (priced on recovered revenue, not seat count); led go-to-market with **one trade, one
> metro** instead of broad outreach. Earlier branches that priced per-lead or targeted all
> home-service trades were explored and dropped.

---

## 1. The setup — who it's for and why now

Single and small-group dental practices (one to four chairs) that still run reception by hand. When
the front desk is on another call, at lunch, or gone for the day, new-patient calls go to voicemail,
and a good share of those callers just dial the next practice. The owner-dentist feels the gap but
has no clean way to staff around it.

You can build the automation. The wedge is not "AI for dentists" in general. It is one specific,
named outcome: **stop losing new patients to missed and after-hours calls.** Why now: voice models
are finally good enough to handle a real intake conversation, and practices are short-staffed enough
to want it.

---

## 2. What you sell — the offer

**"Never miss a new patient again."** A done-for-you AI receptionist that answers the practice's
overflow and after-hours calls, screens for new-patient intent, books the appointment straight into
the existing scheduler, and texts the front desk a summary the next morning.

- One named outcome, fixed scope, flat monthly price.
- Live in five business days. No long build, no per-call metering for the customer.
- You own the setup and the playbook, so each new practice runs the same way.

---

## 3. What you charge — packaging and price

One tier to start: a **setup fee plus a flat monthly retainer**, priced against the revenue a single
recovered new patient represents to the practice, not against your hours or per-lead.

| Item | Price | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | $1,500 one-time | Configure intake script, scheduler hookup, go-live |
| Retainer | $750 / month | Answering, booking, daily summary, light changes |

Anchor the conversation on value: ask the dentist what one new patient is worth to them over a year.
If a recovered patient is worth four figures, a flat monthly that recovers even one extra per month
pays for itself. **Model this per practice. Do not quote the vendor "lifetime value" figures below as
fact.**

---

## 4. How you get customers — go-to-market

Pick **one trade and one metro** and go deep before going wide: independent dental practices in a
single city.

- Build one reference practice first. Offer the first install at a discount in exchange for a
  testimonial and a referral introduction.
- Demo by calling the practice's own after-hours line, letting it ring out, then showing the AI
  handle the same call. The gap sells itself.
- Get the first paying customer before building anything else. Outreach is direct: 50 owner-operators
  in the metro, by phone and in person, not a funnel.

---

## 5. How you deliver — the delivery playbook

Templatize every step so it runs the same at one practice or ten:

1. **Discovery** — capture the practice's hours, scheduler, and intake questions (30 minutes).
2. **Build** — configure the intake script and connect the scheduler.
3. **Test** — run live test calls until booking is clean.
4. **Go-live** — switch over the overflow and after-hours numbers.
5. **Monthly** — a short "what it caught" report: calls answered, appointments booked.

Where delivery breaks at ten clients: scheduler integrations vary by practice. Keep a per-scheduler
checklist so each new one is a known job, not a custom project.

---

## 6. Your first 30 days — the roadmap

- **Week 1** — Build the reference install at one practice. Record the after-hours demo.
- **Week 2** — List 50 practices in the metro. Start outreach with the demo.
- **Week 3** — Run demos and pilots. Convert the first paying retainer.
- **Week 4** — Deliver, collect the first monthly report, and ask for one referral.

## Appendix — research, graded

The source-credibility gate grades every claim. Cited = a primary or neutral source cleared it.
Vendor = a self-interested source; treat as a marketing claim until verified.

| Claim | Grade | Source note |
|---|---|---|
| ~2.5M home-service-style small businesses operate in the US, dental among the local-service set | ✅ cited | census.gov — primary source, passed the gate |
| A large share of inbound calls to small practices go unanswered during busy or off hours | ⚠️ vendor | answering-service vendor blog — self-interested, unverified |
| "Each missed call is worth $X in lifetime patient value" | ⚠️ vendor | call-tracking vendor — directional only; model per practice |

**Read:** the market-size claim is solid. The "missed calls" and "lifetime value" figures come from
companies that sell exactly this, so the plan prices on the dentist's own number, not on the vendor's.
