# Pricing a productized service

Price on the outcome, not the hour. For a productized service, a fixed monthly or per-project fee beats
hourly billing: it decouples your income from your time, makes the buyer's cost predictable, and stops
punishing you for getting faster. Hourly billing caps your upside at your calendar.

Anchor with three tiers. Most buyers pick the middle option, so design the middle tier to be the one
you actually want to sell, and make the top tier expensive enough to make the middle look reasonable.
The cheapest tier exists to be a floor, not to be bought.

Charge for the expensive problem, not the cheap deliverable. A report is cheap; the decision the report
de-risks is expensive. Name the pain in dollars ("you are losing leads worth X per month") and price as
a fraction of that, so the fee reads as a return, not a cost.

Raise prices on new customers first. Existing customers anchor on what they already pay, so test a
higher price on new buyers before touching current ones; the new-customer close rate tells you whether
the market accepts the increase without risking your base.
