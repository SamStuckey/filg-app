# Go-to-market for a solo operator

Pick one narrow beachhead. A solo operator cannot outspend anyone on reach, so win on specificity:
one industry, one job title, one painful problem. "Bookkeeping for dental practices in one metro" beats
"bookkeeping for small businesses" because the pitch, the referrals, and the case studies all compound
inside a tight niche instead of scattering.

Lead with a proof asset, not a pitch. Before asking for a call, show one concrete thing: a teardown of
the prospect's current setup, a short audit, a worked example for a lookalike client. The asset does the
selling and pre-qualifies the buyer, so the call is a decision, not a demo.

Start with owned outreach before paid channels. Direct outreach (email, DMs, warm intros) costs time,
not money, and gives you fast feedback on the message. Only move to paid acquisition once a channel of
manual outreach reliably converts, so you are scaling something that works rather than buying clicks
into a message that does not.

Measure one leading indicator per week. For early GTM the useful number is not revenue, it is a leading
signal: qualified conversations booked, or proof assets sent. Pick one, watch it weekly, and change the
message when it stalls.
